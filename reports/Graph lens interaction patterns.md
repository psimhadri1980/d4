# Compile every lens to one triple-based spec

The best research-backed design for D4 Graph Studio lenses is to stop treating a lens as "contour type plus filters at depth N". Treat it instead as a declarative spec that every visual pattern compiles to. The spec has an **anchor** (a search hit, a picked node, or roots from a reviewed catalog query), a chain of **metamodel triples with direction and depth**, an **idiom** (ego graph, layered flow, nested boxes, matrix, indented tree, path list, diff), a **disclosure policy with an explicit node budget** ranked by degree-of-interest, a **grouping relation** that can be expanded or drilled into, and **encodings** bound to attributes. The academic grounding for this design is van Ham & Perer's "search, show context, expand on demand" model, the matrix-versus-node-link evidence (Ghoniem et al.) and the comparison and mental-map studies. Product convergence points the same way. Ardoq viewpoints are already chains of (source type, reference type, target type) triples. LeanIX reports are parameterised over base type, cluster relation and colour view. Lineage tools (DataHub, Atlan, Collibra, Unity Catalog) all use upstream-left/downstream-right layouts with "+" expanders and hard caps. Neo4j Bloom and Linkurious show how experts wrap parameterised queries as named phrases and context-menu verbs. Because D4 forbids raw Cypher, the triple spec does double duty: it is the authoring surface for users and the safe compilation target that `packages/cypher` can check. The rest of this report ranks the patterns, defines that common model, and sets out the interaction vocabulary and authoring ladder that sit on top of it. One caveat: much of the evidence comes from abstracts and vendor documentation rather than controlled studies, and no source measured how easy view authoring is.

## Thirty years of research converge on search-first, budgeted disclosure

The task literature gives the vocabulary for deciding what a lens is for. Lee et al. sort graph tasks into **topology, attribute, browsing and overview** groups ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/task-taxonomy-graph-visualization/)). Yi et al. name seven interaction intents: **select, explore, reconfigure, encode, abstract/elaborate, filter, connect** ([Stasko notes](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/7450/13/Notes/interaction.pdf)). Heer & Shneiderman add a "process and provenance" layer (record, annotate, share), and that layer is the academic case for saved, shareable lens states ([ACM Queue](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=2146416)). A lens can therefore be described as task + intent + idiom. An impact lens, for example, is a topology task, uses the connect and elaborate intents, and is drawn as a layered flow.

For enterprise graphs the decisive result is that Shneiderman's "overview first" breaks at scale. Van Ham & Perer reverse it: **search, show context, expand on demand**. They adapt Furnas' degree-of-interest (DOI) function to graphs and demonstrate it on a legal-citation graph of **more than 3 million documents** ([researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/HamP09)). The DOI idea scores each node as a-priori importance plus user interest minus distance from the focus. It uses only generic signals (type weight, degree, search hits, pins, hop distance), so it stays metamodel-agnostic. The top-down alternative is ASK-GraphView's cluster drill-down, which reached 16 million edges. Its authors report that **users lose context in deep cluster hierarchies** ([PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/ask-graphview-a-large-scale-graph-visualization-system-4a2xp4v4yh.pdf)), which is the case for always showing a breadcrumb of the drill path.

Idiom choice has firmer evidence than most UI debates. Ghoniem, Fekete & Castagliola found that **above about 20 vertices, adjacency matrices beat node-link diagrams on most tasks, except path finding** ([HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00343819)). For directed graphs, node-link wins on path and cycle tasks ([GD 2014 poster](https://mozart.diei.unipg.it/gd/gd2014/data/uploads/posters/gd14-poster-1.pdf)). NodeTrix reconciles the two by embedding matrices for dense communities inside a sparse node-link layout ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.0599v2)). For connections, Pathfinder shows **a ranked list of paths with attributes, linked to a small node-link context**, with results streamed in incrementally, rather than drawing the whole graph ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5146994)). Juniper's indented tree aligned with an attribute table is the matching answer for multivariate neighbourhoods and containment ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.03261v2)).

On stability and change, Archambault, Purchase & Pinaud found **small multiples significantly faster than animation**. Animation was more accurate for spotting what was added together, and preserving the mental map had little measured effect on time or errors ([Monash](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/animation-small-multiples-and-the-effect-of-mental-map-preservati/)). Follow-up work found that mental-map preservation helps orientation and memorability, while over-strict stability that causes node overlap hurts readability ([Glasgow](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/66915)). For diffs, Gleicher's taxonomy of **juxtaposition, superposition and explicit encoding** frames the options ([Wisconsin](https://pages.graphics.cs.wisc.edu/VisSnacks/papers/comparison)). Recent reviews say juxtaposition holds up better than the old consensus held ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.00192)). The practical rules follow from these results. Pin existing nodes on expand. Animate and highlight only the new ones. Make re-layout an explicit action. Draw diffs as explicit added, removed and changed marks over one stable layout.

## A ranked catalog of eleven lens patterns

The ranking below weighs three things: how often an enterprise question needs the pattern, how strong the evidence is, and how cleanly it compiles to the common model. Rank 1 is the default D4 should ship first. The use-case column draws on the sourced viewpoints, lineage and BCBS 239 material. The banking examples are assembled from cited features rather than vendor-documented recipes, because the notes found no banking-specific vendor examples.

Some cross-cutting modifiers apply to every pattern rather than being patterns themselves. **Histogram and range filters** cover attribute tasks: Bloom's slicer ([blog](https://neo4j.com/?p=362261)) and Graphistry's click-a-bin filter ([docs](https://graphistry.infra-prod.blackbird.ai/docs/ui/histograms)). **Path focus by dimming** comes from Atlan's visibility slider. **Permission masking** follows Unity Catalog, where nodes the user may not browse appear masked and cannot be expanded past. **Edge provenance** follows DataHub, where manually added edges carry an avatar and a timestamp. The "gap" lens, such as Ardoq's capabilities with no linked applications ([help](https://help.ardoq.com/en/articles/44074-gremlin-graph-search-examples)), maps directly onto D4's existing "not linked" facet.

## One lens model that every pattern compiles to

The common model generalises today's D4 lens without discarding it. Today's contour type becomes the anchor's type constraint, the attribute filters become anchor predicates, the linked/not-linked facets become existence constraints on a step, and depth N becomes a per-step depth. The fields below are synthesised from Ardoq triples, LeanIX report parameters, dbt selectors and the DOI literature. The exact names are a recommendation, not a sourced standard.

| Field | Contents | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| **anchor** | One of: search hit, picked node(s), the type plus attribute filters plus facets (today's lens), or roots from a reviewed catalog query referenced by id. Never Cypher text | Bloom search-first ([docs](https://neo4j.com/docs/bloom-user-guide/current/bloom-visual-tour/search-bar/)); van Ham & Perer ([researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/HamP09)) |
| **steps[]** | Each step is `{source type, relationship kind, target type, direction: out/in/both, minDepth..maxDepth, optional target filter, exists/not-exists}`. Steps chain so the types connect; an entry point can be any step | Ardoq triples ([help](https://help.ardoq.com/en/articles/43962-understanding-viewpoints)); dbt `n+root+m` ([docs](https://docs.getdbt.com/reference/node-selection/graph-operators)) |
| **idiom** | ego, layered-flow (with a direction axis), nested-box, matrix (row and column bindings), tree-table, path-list, timeline, diff | Ghoniem ([HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00343819)); LeanIX ([docs](https://docs-eam.leanix.net/docs/reports)) |
| **disclosure** | Initial hops, a node/edge budget (for example 150 nodes, with a hard stop near Collibra's 350 nodes / 1,000 edges), a per-node expand cap, a "show more" page size (Atlan prompts at 100), visible truncation markers | Collibra ([docs](https://docs.collibra.com/Content/CollibraDataLineage/ref_technical-lineage-graph.htm)); Atlan ([docs](https://docs.atlan.com/product/capabilities/lineage/how-tos/view-lineage.md)) |
| **interest (DOI)** | Score = type weight from the metamodel + degree or centrality + user signals (search match, pin) − hop distance. It decides what fills the budget and the order "expand" adds nodes in | Furnas / van Ham & Perer ([researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/HamP09)) |
| **grouping** | A grouping relation or single-select field (must be functional, or a rule for multi-membership); fold same-type siblings into counted glyphs; expand in place or drill in with a breadcrumb | LeanIX ([docs](https://docs-eam.leanix.net/docs/how-to-use-leanix-reports)); KeyLines ([CI](https://cambridge-intelligence.com/?p=7419)); ASK-GraphView ([PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/ask-graphview-a-large-scale-graph-visualization-system-4a2xp4v4yh.pdf)) |
| **encodings** | Colour, size, label, badge and border bound to attributes or relationship presence (conditional formatting); a legend generated from the bindings | Bloom rule-based styling ([brief](https://go.neo4j.com/rs/710-RRC-335/images/Neo4j_Product_Brief_Bloom.pdf)); Ardoq perspectives ([help](https://help.ardoq.com/en/articles/44111-saved-perspectives)) |
| **actions[]** | Named verbs, each gated by selection type, that open another lens with the selection as its anchor | Bloom scene actions ([blog](https://neo4j.com/?p=190025)); Linkurious templates ([blog](https://linkurious.com/blog/top-10-query-templates-to-accelerate-your-investigations/)) |
| **time / layer** | Optional "as of" range (fade outside it) and granularity layer | ReGraph ([notes](https://developers.cambridge-intelligence.com/downloads/release-notes/regraph/v1)); Manta ([IBM](https://ibm.com/support/pages/system/files/inline-files/MANTA%20Flow%20Visualization%20User%20Documentation.pdf)) |

This mirrors the 42010 split: a lens definition is a **viewpoint**, which is reusable and defined on metamodel concepts, and a lens opened on a given anchor is a **view** ([Eclipse Papyrus wiki](https://wiki.eclipse.org/Papyrus/Oxygen_Work_Description/NewFeature/PapyrusAFViewpointSwitch)). The server compiles steps into parameterised, bounded traversals validated against the metamodel. Users therefore express rich traversals without ever supplying query text, a deliberate departure from Ardoq's raw Gremlin console and Bloom's Cypher-backed phrases. Two design consequences follow. First, the metamodel's relationship kinds and their direction must be first-class and discoverable, because they are the lens vocabulary. Second, every step needs a depth ceiling the server enforces. The notes do not establish safe default depths for D4's graphs, so the budget numbers above are starting points to tune, not proven values.

## A shared vocabulary of twelve verbs across all lenses

Products converge on a small set of verbs that should behave identically in every idiom. **Search** is one box that merges saved phrases, pattern suggestions built from schema vocabulary, full-text and commands; Bloom's search bar does exactly this, building its vocabulary from labels, relationship types and indexed values ([docs](https://neo4j.com/docs/bloom-user-guide/current/bloom-visual-tour/search-bar/)). **Expand** adds neighbours by relationship kind and direction, with a count shown before clicking (DataHub's expand buttons show dependency counts) and a cap. **Collapse/group** and **ungroup/drill** fold and unfold, using an incremental layout that keeps existing positions, as yFiles' fold-expand demos do ([yFiles](https://docs.yworks.com/yfiles/doc/demo/layout/hierarchic/IncrementalHierarchicFoldExpandDemo.java.html)). **Focus/refocus** re-anchors on a selection, and **path-focus** dims everything off the selected path. **Filter** covers facet chips, histograms and range sliders. **Dismiss** and **dismiss others** prune the scene. **Pin** feeds DOI and freezes position. **Undo/redo** and a **breadcrumb** carry history. **Switch idiom** flips the same result between graph, table, tree and matrix (Paradime's Graph/Table toggle ([docs](https://docs.paradime.io/products/data-catalog/lineage/column-level-lineage))). **Open as** runs a selection-gated action into another lens. **Export** produces the impact table as CSV and the image.

Two trust behaviours belong in the vocabulary as well. **Truncation is always visible**: Collibra marks truncated connections with an icon rather than dropping them silently. **Hover details** follow Atlan's popover with owners, certification and DQ. Making these verbs uniform is what makes lenses metamodel-agnostic in practice, because users learn one grammar and every new relationship kind simply appears in the menu for expand, filter and group.

## Authoring climbs a five-rung ladder from search phrase to viewpoint

The products show that easy authoring is layered, with different users on different rungs. On rung one, users **save the current state** as a perspective: anchor, filters, grouping and styling. Ardoq perspectives and Bloom perspectives both work this way ([Ardoq](https://help.ardoq.com/en/articles/44111-saved-perspectives), [Neo4j](https://neo4j.com/?p=327759)). On rung two, users run **templates with typed parameters**. Linkurious turns queries into buttons and forms run from a right-clicked node, shared with the owner shown ([Linkurious](https://linkurious.com/blog/linkurious-enterprise-2-10-more-expressive-visualizations-powerful-query-templates-and-improved-indexing-strategies/)). In D4 these are lens definitions with anchor slots, not Cypher templates. On rung three, users type **search phrases**: named, parameterised aliases ("impact of $application", "CDEs feeding $report") that bind to a lens and its anchor. This is Bloom's search phrase without the embedded Cypher. Reviewed catalog queries supply the roots where needed.

On rung four, a **wizard or no-code builder** picks the anchor type, adds steps by choosing from the relationship kinds valid for the current type (autocomplete from the metamodel), sets direction and depth, then the idiom, grouping and colour, all **with a live preview**. Linkurious' No-Code Query Builder previews results as the query is refined ([Linkurious](https://linkurious.com/blog/no-code-query-builder/)). Metaphactory wizards generate a configuration that experts can then edit by hand ([metaphacts](https://blog.metaphacts.com/building-a-knowledge-graph-application-is-easier-than-ever-with-metaphactory-s-intuitive-wizards)). On rung five, admins publish **viewpoints** with a default per type, which is Ardoq's default viewpoint per component type ([Ardoq](https://help.ardoq.com/en/articles/43924-how-to-set-up-a-default-viewpoint-for-a-component-type)), and a compact text form for power users modelled on dbt's `2+app+3` notation ([dbt](https://docs.getdbt.com/reference/node-selection/graph-operators)), compiled to the same spec. Ardoq's own starter viewpoints are a ready seed set for D4: application lifecycle and ownership, capability realisation, integration owners and lineage, and cost roll-ups ([Ardoq](https://help.ardoq.com/en/articles/176967-five-commonly-used-viewpoints-in-ardoq-and-how-to-create-them)).

For credit-risk and data-governance work, one authored lens matters most. Anchor it on a report line or CDE (RWA, PD, LGD), traverse upstream to data capture, and annotate each hop with owner, manual-step flag and DQ control status. Pair the graph with an exportable evidence table. This matches the ECB RDARR expectation of **complete, up-to-date, attribute-level, end-to-end lineage** that includes manual steps, owners and controls, and the ECB's rejection of ad hoc lineage ([Solidatus on ECB](https://www.solidatus.com/blog/detailed-expectations-around-data-lineage-and-bcbs-239-from-the-european-central-bank-ecb/)). It also matches Collibra's advice to prioritise CDEs over mapping everything ([Collibra](https://www.collibra.com/blog/bcbs-239-explained-how-banks-can-prove-data-integrity-to-regulators)). The BCBS 239 text itself never says "lineage"; supervisors read it in from the outcome principles ([DataHub](https://datahub.com/?p=15324)), and the primary ECB text was not consulted directly.

## Conclusion

The most useful shift for D4 is conceptual. The visual idioms are interchangeable renderers, and the durable asset is a small, checkable traversal spec built from metamodel triples. That spec is what lets "search phrase", "template", "wizard" and "viewpoint" be four editors over one object rather than four features. It is also what keeps the no-raw-Cypher rule from limiting expressiveness, because the server compiles bounded, validated traversals from it. Ardoq and LeanIX prove the metamodel-defined approach for architecture. The lineage vendors prove budgets and expanders at scale. Bloom and Linkurious prove the expert-packages, analyst-explores split.

The open questions are empirical, not architectural. The notes found no studies of how easy authoring is, no usage statistics on which idioms enterprises rely on, and no node-count threshold for usable node-link views beyond the roughly 20-node matrix crossover. D4 should therefore treat its budgets, DOI weights and default depths as instrumented parameters, logging truncations, expands and idiom switches per lens. That way the first release of the lens model also gathers the evidence the literature lacks.
